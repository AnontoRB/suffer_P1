60-Day Strength, Water and Food Plan

Nov 8, 2026 to Jan 6, 2027. This plan sits on your running rest days. You run on Nov 9, 11, 13 and so on, and you do strength work on the days in between, so every day is either a run or a strength day. It starts with your current numbers (8 push-ups, 30 sit-ups, 30 squats, no pull-ups) and gets harder in four blocks. It ends with a rest day on Jan 5 and the race on Jan 6.

What to expect (honest version)

In 60 days of consistent work, a realistic result is: push-ups from 8 to about 20 or more, a plank hold of 90 seconds or more, 65+ squats and sit-ups, slow controlled pull-up negatives (a first full pull-up is possible but not guaranteed), a smaller waist, better posture, and the ability to run 7.5 km. Fat loss of roughly 3 to 6 kg is realistic if you follow the food and sleep advice, but it varies by person. You will look and feel clearly different, which is not the same as being unrecognizable, and the plan is built to get you as far as is safe.

The four blocks

Day 0 (today, Nov 8): baseline

  • Weigh yourself first thing in the morning, after the toilet, before eating.
  • Measure with a tape: waist (at the belly button), chest, upper arm and thigh. Write the numbers in the measurements table below.
  • Take front, side and back photos in the same clothes and lighting. Repeat on Dec 10 and Jan 7.
  • Test: plank hold (max time) and dead hang (max time, only if you have a bar). Write the numbers down.
  • Read the exercise guide below so you know the movements before tomorrow.

Session structure (about 35 to 45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min): march in place 1 min, 20 arm circles, 10 leg swings per leg, 5 cat-cows, 5 slow squats, 5 easy incline push-ups.
  2. Main work: the day's list. Rest 60 to 90 seconds between sets (2 minutes for push-ups in Block 1).
  3. Cooldown (5 min): hold each stretch 30 seconds: calves, hamstrings, quads, hip flexors, chest.

Daily habits

  • Brush your teeth twice a day: once in the morning and once at night.
  • Take a shower every day.

All three are on every day's checklist, so they count towards finishing the day.

Rules

  • Stop most sets with 1 to 2 reps left, not at total failure.
  • Move to the next variation only when you hit the top reps in every set.
  • If a session feels impossible, repeat the previous session's numbers instead.
  • Your legs also have to run the next day. If they are very sore, drop one set from every leg exercise.
  • Sharp joint pain (not just tired muscles) means stop that exercise. At your weight, never do jumping moves.
  • If you have heart, joint or blood-pressure concerns, check with a doctor before starting.

Exercise guide

Day-by-day strength schedule

On A days you focus on pushing, legs and core. On B days you focus on pulling, glutes and core. Test days are explained below.

Checkpoint tests

On test days do the tests first, with 3 minutes of rest between them, then the short session listed. Do each test with strict form (chest near floor, squats to parallel). Type your results in the boxes.

Body measurements

Also weigh yourself every Sunday morning under the same conditions. Judge progress by the weekly trend, not by day-to-day swings. Sundays have a weigh-in box in their daily log.

Water plan

  • Goals: until Nov 24: 2.5 L on strength days, 3.0 L on run days. From Nov 25: 3.0 L and 3.5 L. Drink more if you sweat a lot, and never force more than about 4.5 L.
  • Easy math: one glass is 250 ml, so 3 L is 12 glasses.
  • Schedule: 1 glass on waking, 1 before each meal, 1 mid-morning, 1 mid-afternoon, 1 in the evening, and the rest spread out. Drink 300 to 500 ml in the 2 hours before a run, and sip after it.
  • Check: pale yellow urine means you are drinking enough.
  • Jan 5 and Jan 6: drink steadily, don't gulp. On race day have about 500 ml 2 to 3 hours before the start.

Food plan

The goal is slow, steady fat loss while keeping your energy for running and strength work. Aim for 0.3 to 0.5 kg per week. If you lose faster than about 0.7 kg per week, eat more.

  • Protein: about 110 to 130 g a day (eggs, chicken, fish, dal and lentils, yogurt, paneer, beans).
  • Calories (rough guide): about 2,200 to 2,500 kcal a day, higher on run days. Adjust using your weekly weight trend.
  • Plate method: half the plate vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter carbs (rice, roti, oats, potatoes). Fill the last bit with healthy fats.
  • Before a run: a light carb snack 1 to 2 hours before, such as a banana or toast.
  • After a run or workout: protein plus carbs within 1 to 2 hours, such as eggs and toast, or rice, dal and chicken.
  • Limit: sugary drinks, fried snacks and late-night eating.
  • Sleep: 7 to 8 hours. Muscle is built while you sleep.
  • Rust study: stand up and walk for 5 minutes each hour while you code.

After the race

On Jan 7, repeat your measurements and photos, and compare them with Nov 8. Take 2 or 3 easy days, then pick the next goal: a faster 7.5 km, a 10 km run, or your first pull-up.
