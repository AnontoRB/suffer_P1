Progressive overload guide

Your body only improves when the work gets slightly harder than last time.

  • Change one thing at a time: reps, sets, slower tempo, or shorter rest. Never all at once.
  • Double progression: keep a rep target until every set feels easy, then add 1–2 reps.
  • Leave 2 reps in the tank in weeks 1–2. Stop before your form breaks.
  • Slow the lowering phase to 3 seconds on push-ups and squats; this makes the same reps much harder.
  • Cut rest from 90 s toward 60 s as sets get easier.
  • Push-up ladder: knee push-ups → full push-ups → slow full push-ups → hands on the floor with feet raised on a step → pike push-ups.
  • Squat ladder: squats → slow squats → reverse lunges → split squats → single-leg sit-to-stand.
  • Running: the 10% rule. Raise weekly running time by no more than about 10% once you are past this plan.
  • Pull-ups: you can't train them with zero equipment. Superman holds build your back for now. If you get access to a bar, start with hanging and slow lowering.
  • Log everything. If you can't see last week's numbers, you can't beat them.
  • Soreness is normal; sharp or joint pain is not. Shin pain that worsens while running means rest and reduce volume.

After day 30: toward January 6

Be honest about the distance. A full marathon (42.2 km) is a very big step from a standing start. Most first-time plans use 16–20 weeks, and you have about 8. If your race is a 5K or 10K, this plan puts you on track. If it is a full or half marathon, plan to run/walk it, build long sessions gradually (roughly 50, 65, then 80 minutes over the following weeks, then taper in the final week), and consider a shorter distance if your body isn't coping. Water and sleep rules

  • Water: 3 L a day is the goal (adjust up in hot weather or on run days). Drink a glass on waking and one before each meal.
  • Sleep: aim for 8 hours, for example in bed at 22:30 and up at 06:30. Keep the same times every day, including rest days.
  • Recovery: muscle and endurance gains happen while you sleep, so protect sleep before adding workouts.
General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Stop if you feel chest pain, dizziness, or sharp pain.
